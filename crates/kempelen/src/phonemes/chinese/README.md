# Mandarin pronunciation

Reads Chinese the way Kokoro's Chinese voices were trained to hear it. [mod.rs](mod.rs) lists the steps,
[reader.rs](reader.rs) does them, [numbers.rs](numbers.rs) spells Arabic numerals in Chinese and
[phonemizer.rs](phonemizer.rs) plugs the reader into narration as a `Phonemizer`.

The readings are data, not code: `crates/kempelen/mandarin/{characters,phrases,syllables}.tsv` are generated by
[export.py](../../../mandarin/export.py) from pypinyin and misaki, so the Rust side needs no Python. To refresh
them (new pypinyin, new misaki), follow the commands at the top of `export.py` and
`tests/golden/misaki_reference.py`, then run `cargo test -p kempelen --lib chinese`: the test
`mandarin_matches_misaki` requires every sentence of the recorded corpus (hand-written lines, traditional
characters, numbers, dates and 400 random sentences built from pypinyin's phrases) to come out exactly as
misaki's own `ZHG2P` gives it.

Deliberate differences from misaki, none of which the corpus covers: strings of digits that are identifiers
(a leading zero, or 11 or more digits) are read digit by digit; a hyphen between digits is a range, not a
minus sign; thousands separators (`1,234`) are understood; fullwidth digits are read; and English words in
Chinese text are read as American English by jespersen (what it cannot read is left out) where misaki
leaves the letters as they are.

Traditional text (Taiwan, Hong Kong) is first written in Simplified characters (`to_simplified`, OpenCC's character
table, plus a rule that reads 著 as the particle 着 except in the words that read "zhù"), because the word cutter and
pronunciation dictionaries know Simplified text best. Hong Kong text is read in Mandarin: the voices cannot speak
Cantonese.

Not done, as in the misaki path Kokoro v1.0 uses: tone sandhi (a third tone before another third tone
is spoken as a second tone). The newer misaki "1.1" front end does it and belongs to the Kokoro 1.1 Chinese
model, which kempelen does not use.
